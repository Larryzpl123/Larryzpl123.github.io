Computational Neuroscience · preprint

Screens Reject, Only Mechanism Confirms: Recovering Emergent Gamma in a Reduced Hippocampal CA1 Microcircuit

Independent research. It shows the standard spectral test is blind to genuine gamma on a theta-driven CA1 network, builds a comb-notch statistic to recover it, and confirms emergent gamma with a pre-registered PING test, reversing the study's own earlier conclusion. Python, with full code. Zenodo preprint.

Brain-Computer Interfaces · preprint

Diagnosing Motor-Imagery BCI Failure: A Three-Step Diagnostic Framework

Independent research on why motor-imagery BCIs fail. A three-step diagnostic: classDis (Lotte & Jeunet, 2018) measures class separability before any decoder is trained, a transfer gate separates subjects who decode within a session but not across one, and filter-bank CSP, a Riemannian classifier and permutation tests confirm what remains. Pooled across three cohorts (75 subjects), classDis tracks cross-session decoding at r = .66. Python, with full code. Zenodo preprint.

Computational Neuroscience · preprint

Smooth Surrogate Models Approve Impossible Firing Patterns: A Hidden Flaw in Writing Brain Codes

Independent research on the write side of a neural interface. Optimising stimulation against a smooth, differentiable surrogate of a neuron's rate-current curve certifies target firing rates the real cell cannot produce: the surrogate reports zero error while the neuron stays silent. Whether that happens is set by the cell's onset bifurcation class, and a feasibility screen against the true curve removes it. Python, with full code. Zenodo preprint.

U.S. History · research paper

McCarthyism's Effects on Academic Freedom and Higher Education

A year-long research paper arguing that the Second Red Scare damaged the American university less through congressional pressure than through administrative capitulation: loyalty oaths and HUAC hearings did the visible work, but it was universities policing themselves that made the damage stick. Institutions failed the value they claimed to exist for.

History · John Locke 2025 (Shortlisted)

Beyond Shame and Pride: A Balanced Approach to National History

National shame and national pride are usually treated as opposites. I argue they fail the same way: both replace historical understanding with an emotional verdict, and shame in particular paralyses the civic engagement it is meant to produce. What works instead looks like a truth commission, aimed at accuracy and reconciliation rather than punishment or celebration. Shortlisted, John Locke Global Essay Competition.

History · John Locke 2026 (Shortlisted)

The Most Famous Stranger in the World: Does Che Deserve His Iconic T-Shirt?

The prompt asks whether Che "deserves" the T-shirt. I argue the question smuggles in a premise: that the shirt is a verdict on the man. It stopped pointing at him decades ago. The essay weighs the executions honestly, citing a contested range rather than one convenient number, and then turns the image around: what the shirt reveals is the wearer, and a culture that no longer asks what the face on its chest is supposed to mean.

Psychology · John Locke 2026 (Shortlisted)

The Thermometer That Changes the Weather: Is Mental Illness Over-Diagnosed or Better Recognized?

Over-diagnosis or better recognition? The evidence supports both, and I defend that mixture. But settling the proportions would not settle the dispute, because both answers share a hidden premise: that a diagnosis only describes. It also acts. The threshold for "clinically significant" is a judgment, not a measurement, and where it falls decides whose hard life becomes a condition. Shortlisted, John Locke Global Essay Competition.
