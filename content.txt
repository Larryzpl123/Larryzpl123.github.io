=== ABOUT ===
I work at the intersection of **neuroscience, computing, and history**. Three of my projects turn out to ask one question: *when does a measurement confidently report something that isn't there?* A hippocampal CA1 model where the spectral statistic found a rhythm that was an artifact of its own input. A brain-computer interface where the hard part was telling a decoder's failure apart from the signal's. An EEG rig I rebuilt from one electrode to twenty-two after tracing its failure to the hardware rather than the code.

The question does not stay in the lab. I spent a year on a paper arguing that McCarthyism damaged American universities less through congressional pressure than through administrative capitulation, institutions failing the value they claimed to exist for. In an essay on psychiatric diagnosis I argued that the threshold for *clinically significant* is a judgment, not a measurement, and that where it falls decides whose hard life becomes a condition. I build historical arguments in **Civilization VI** mods too, where a claim about what a leader optimized for has to be written as numbers and can then be proved wrong by play.

I also build things that simply have to work: a dining-hall ordering system for my school, and a rating engine that turned my dorm's foosball table into a social hub. What drives me is curiosity about the brain: how it computes, remembers, and might one day interface with machines, and I care about using what I build to help people, from a scholarship-funded housing project for rural families to reading with kids.

**Computational neuroscience**: biologically constrained microcircuit models, model simplification, and neural oscillations (theta / gamma).

=== WORKS ===
TAG: Computational Neuroscience · preprint
TITLE: Screens Reject, Only Mechanism Confirms: Recovering Emergent Gamma in a Reduced Hippocampal CA1 Microcircuit
LINK: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21272958
DESCRIPTION: Independent research. It shows the standard spectral test is blind to genuine gamma on a theta-driven CA1 network, builds a comb-notch statistic to recover it, and confirms emergent gamma with a pre-registered PING test, reversing the study's own earlier conclusion. Python, with full code. *Zenodo preprint.*

TAG: Brain-Computer Interfaces · preprint
TITLE: Diagnosing Motor-Imagery BCI Failure: A Three-Step Diagnostic Framework
LINK: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21538627
DESCRIPTION: Independent research on why motor-imagery BCIs fail. A three-step diagnostic: classDis (Lotte & Jeunet, 2018) measures class separability before any decoder is trained, a transfer gate separates subjects who decode within a session but not across one, and filter-bank CSP, a Riemannian classifier and permutation tests confirm what remains. Pooled across three cohorts (75 subjects), classDis tracks cross-session decoding at r = .66. Python, with full code. *Zenodo preprint.*

TAG: Computational Neuroscience · preprint
TITLE: Smooth Surrogate Models Approve Impossible Firing Patterns: A Hidden Flaw in Writing Brain Codes
LINK: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21645655
DESCRIPTION: Independent research on the write side of a neural interface. Optimising stimulation against a smooth, differentiable surrogate of a neuron's rate-current curve certifies target firing rates the real cell cannot produce: the surrogate reports zero error while the neuron stays silent. Whether that happens is set by the cell's onset bifurcation class, and a feasibility screen against the true curve removes it. Python, with full code. *Zenodo preprint.*

TAG: History · John Locke 2025 (Shortlisted)
TITLE: Beyond Shame and Pride: A Balanced Approach to National History
LINK: /works/history-2025.pdf
DESCRIPTION: National shame and national pride are usually treated as opposites. I argue they fail the same way: both replace historical understanding with an emotional verdict, and shame in particular paralyses the civic engagement it is meant to produce. What works instead looks like a truth commission, aimed at accuracy and reconciliation rather than punishment or celebration. Shortlisted, John Locke Global Essay Competition.

TAG: History · John Locke 2026 (Shortlisted)
TITLE: The Most Famous Stranger in the World: Does Che Deserve His Iconic T-Shirt?
LINK: /works/history-2026.pdf
DESCRIPTION: The prompt asks whether Che "deserves" the T-shirt. I argue the question smuggles in a premise: that the shirt is a verdict on the man. It stopped pointing at him decades ago. The essay weighs the executions honestly, citing a contested range rather than one convenient number, and then turns the image around: what the shirt reveals is the wearer, and a culture that no longer asks what the face on its chest is supposed to mean.

TAG: Psychology · John Locke 2026 (Shortlisted)
TITLE: The Thermometer That Changes the Weather: Is Mental Illness Over-Diagnosed or Better Recognized?
LINK: /works/psych-2026.pdf
DESCRIPTION: Over-diagnosis or better recognition? The evidence supports both, and I defend that mixture. But settling the proportions would not settle the dispute, because both answers share a hidden premise: that a diagnosis only describes. It also acts. The threshold for "clinically significant" is a judgment, not a measurement, and where it falls decides whose hard life becomes a condition. Shortlisted, John Locke Global Essay Competition.
